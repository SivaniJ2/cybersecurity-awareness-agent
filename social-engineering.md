# Social Engineering

## What is social engineering?

Social engineering is the use of deception or manipulation to influence people into revealing information, providing access, or taking an action that may compromise security.

Instead of attacking a technical system directly, an attacker may try to persuade a person to make a mistake.

## Common social engineering techniques

Examples include:

- **Impersonation:** Pretending to be a trusted person or organization.
- **Urgency:** Creating pressure so someone acts without verifying the request.
- **Authority:** Claiming to be a manager, administrator, technical support person, or other trusted authority.
- **Pretexting:** Creating a believable story or situation to obtain information or access.
- **Phishing:** Using deceptive messages to persuade someone to click a link, open an attachment, or provide information.

## Warning signs

Be cautious when someone:

- Requests sensitive or confidential information unexpectedly.
- Pressures you to act immediately.
- Asks you to bypass normal security procedures.
- Claims to represent an organization but cannot be verified.
- Requests passwords, authentication codes, or other security credentials.
- Provides instructions that seem unusual or inconsistent with normal procedures.

## How to protect yourself

- Stop and think before responding to unexpected requests.
- Verify the person's identity using a trusted method.
- Never share passwords or authentication codes.
- Follow established security procedures.
- Report suspicious activity to the appropriate security or IT team.
