# Phishing

## What is phishing?

Phishing is a type of social engineering in which an attacker sends a deceptive message that appears to come from a trusted person or organization. The goal may be to trick someone into clicking a harmful link, opening an attachment, downloading malicious software, or providing sensitive information.

Phishing can occur through email, text messages, phone calls, social media, and other messaging platforms.

## Common signs of phishing

Be cautious when a message:

- Creates a strong sense of urgency or pressure to act immediately.
- Comes from a suspicious or unexpected sender address.
- Asks for passwords, financial information, or other sensitive information.
- Contains unexpected attachments.
- Contains links that appear suspicious or do not match the expected website.
- Uses unusual greetings, spelling, grammar, or formatting.
- Claims there is a problem with an account and asks you to verify information.

## How to handle suspicious messages

If a message looks suspicious:

1. Stop and think before clicking anything.
2. Do not click suspicious links or open unexpected attachments.
3. Verify the request using contact information you already know to be legitimate.
4. Do not reply to the suspicious message.
5. Report the message using your organization's reporting process or the available spam/phishing reporting feature.
6. Delete the message after reporting it, according to your organization's procedures.

## Suspicious links and attachments

Treat unexpected links and attachments with caution. If you are unsure about a message, verify it through a trusted channel rather than using contact details or links provided in the suspicious message.

## If you already interacted with a phishing message

If you clicked a suspicious link, opened an attachment, or provided sensitive information, report the incident promptly through your organization's incident-response process.

If credentials may have been exposed, change the affected password and follow your organization's security procedures.

## Reporting phishing

Reporting suspected phishing helps organizations identify attacks and protect other users.

Use your organization's designated phishing-reporting mechanism when one is available. For messages that impersonate an organization, use contact information obtained from the organization's legitimate website rather than information contained in the suspicious message.
