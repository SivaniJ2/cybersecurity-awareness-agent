# Incident Reporting

## Why reporting security incidents matters

Reporting suspected security incidents quickly can help an organization investigate the event, contain potential threats, and protect other users and systems.

You do not need to be certain that an event is a security incident before reporting it. If something appears suspicious, follow your organization's reporting process.

## Examples of situations to report

Examples include:

- Suspected phishing messages.
- Unexpected requests for passwords or authentication codes.
- Suspicious links or attachments.
- Unexpected MFA approval requests.
- Suspected exposure of sensitive information.
- Lost or stolen devices that may contain organizational information.
- Suspicious activity on an account or computer.
- Possible malware or other unusual system behavior.

## What to do when reporting

1. Stop interacting with the suspicious message, link, file, or request.
2. Preserve relevant information if your organization's procedures require it.
3. Report the incident using the organization's designated security or IT reporting channel.
4. Provide the information requested by the security or IT team.
5. Follow any additional instructions provided by the organization.

## Do not delay reporting

If you believe an account, device, or sensitive information may have been compromised, report it as soon as possible.

Early reporting can give security teams more time to investigate and respond.
